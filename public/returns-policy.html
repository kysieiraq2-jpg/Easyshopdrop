Returns & Refund Policy

Shop&Drop facilitates transactions between independent buyers, sellers, service providers and transport providers.

This policy explains how returns, complaints, refunds and disputes are handled fairly without treating Shop&Drop as the ordinary seller of independently advertised goods.

Each independent participant remains responsible for their own contractual and applicable legal obligations. Shop&Drop remains responsible for its own intermediary services.

South African launch edition — prelaunch draft. Subject to final legal, administrator and payment-provider approval.

Important: The intended seller payout milestone for physical goods is independently verified courier collection. For Other Services and Dispatching, it is verified completion. These revised payout processes are not yet operational. Nothing in this policy removes mandatory consumer rights or remedies.

1. Scope of This Policy

This policy applies to eligible physical product sales and independent service, transport and dispatch bookings arranged through Shop&Drop in South Africa.

A product sale ordinarily takes place between an independent buyer and seller. A service or transport agreement ordinarily takes place between the customer and the relevant independent provider.

Shop&Drop facilitates these transactions, including advertising, checkout, notifications, courier coordination and transaction references.

Applicable rights, responsibilities and remedies depend on the transaction, the parties, their legal status and South African law.

2. Products Not Received

If a buyer has paid for an item that has not been delivered, the buyer may open a dispute using the relevant Shop&Drop order reference.

A confirmed courier collection establishes evidence that a parcel entered the authorized courier's custody. It does not establish that the parcel was successfully delivered to the buyer.

Where authorized courier collection has been verified, a non-delivery claim will ordinarily involve the responsible courier under its applicable carriage agreement and South African law.

Shop&Drop may assist by providing relevant order, courier booking, tracking and collection records.

Where collection did not occur, or evidence suggests fraudulent collection, the relevant seller, payment and courier records may require investigation.

Courier insurance and compensation depend on actual contractual coverage, exclusions and applicable law. Shop&Drop does not guarantee that every parcel is insured or that every loss is reimbursed in full by a courier.

Where a refund or other remedy is legally due, the responsible party and authorized payment provider must address that obligation as applicable.

Payment already released to a seller does not automatically extinguish a buyer's lawful rights.

3. Damaged, Incorrect or Misrepresented Items

Independent sellers are responsible for the accuracy of their advertisements and the goods they supply.

This includes accurate descriptions, known defects, condition, specifications, authenticity and the item physically handed to the courier.

Customers may report incorrect products, undisclosed material defects, misleading advertisements or goods damaged during transport.

For a complaint about description or condition, Shop&Drop may facilitate communication between the buyer and independent seller.

Shop&Drop does not ordinarily inspect, test or certify independent advertised goods.

Transport damage may involve the courier's custody and applicable carriage obligations. A defect existing before collection may involve the seller's obligations.

Depending on the circumstances and applicable law, remedies may include repair, replacement, return, refund or other lawful relief.

4. Second-Hand and Pre-Owned Products

Shop&Drop welcomes lawful second-hand, homemade, handmade, refurbished and pre-owned goods.

Sellers must describe products truthfully and identify known significant defects or limitations.

Second-hand and handmade items may exhibit normal wear, variations or imperfections.

Buyers should consider the advertised price, age, condition, descriptions and photographs before purchasing.

Normal wear that was clearly disclosed is not necessarily equivalent to a concealed defect or false description.

However, describing goods as used, handmade or sold as-is does not automatically remove statutory rights where they apply.

The particular transaction and whether the seller acts in the ordinary course of business may affect the applicable legal protections.

5. Change-of-Mind Returns

A buyer's change of mind does not create an unconditional refund right for every private transaction.

However, cooling-off, cancellation and return rights may arise under the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act, the Consumer Protection Act or other applicable legislation.

Statutory exceptions may apply, including to certain personalized goods, perishables or services, depending on the circumstances.

Shop&Drop will not treat a mandatory cancellation right as optional simply because a seller has already been paid.

Any voluntary return agreement between buyer and seller must respect rights that cannot lawfully be waived.

6. Services and Service Bookings

For cleaning, pest control, repairs and similar services, the intended payout milestone is verified completion of the agreed service, not merely the provider's arrival.

Providers may report completion through authenticated evidence linked to the correct booking.

Evidence may include completion photographs, relevant time or location records and an appropriate six-digit customer completion PIN.

Photographs alone do not necessarily establish that work was satisfactory. Shop&Drop may use automated checks to establish whether appropriate completion evidence has been submitted.

Customers retain the ability to report genuine non-performance, material defects or false completion claims.

However, a customer's failure to respond should not indefinitely prevent a provider's payment where adequate independent completion evidence exists and no qualifying legal or financial hold applies.

For dispatch and transportation, the relevant milestone ordinarily involves verified completion of the agreed journey and destination handover.

For hourly equipment hire, completion may be established at the end of each agreed paid working period.

Additional working hours or services require a further agreement where applicable.

The final payment release remains subject to verified payment and applicable safeguards.

7. Courier and Transport Charges

Courier charges are intended to be allocated through the authorized payment arrangement, rather than treated as proceeds belonging to the product seller.

Authorized couriers must comply with the relevant collection, custody, carriage, delivery and contractual obligations.

Loss, damage, delay and failed delivery claims may require assessment under the courier agreement, available insurance and applicable law.

The item price, courier charge and marketplace service fee may involve different responsible parties and payment arrangements.

Shop&Drop may facilitate records and communication without guaranteeing a particular insurer's response.

Mandatory refund or compensation rights take precedence over conflicting contractual terms.

8. Shop&Drop Marketplace Fees

Shop&Drop charges a buyer-paid marketplace fee, disclosed before checkout.

Sellers specify the net amount they wish to receive for their products or services.

Where a transaction is refunded, the treatment of Shop&Drop's marketplace fee depends on the reason for the refund, the service supplied, applicable payment-provider charges and mandatory law.

Shop&Drop's two administrators may authorize an exceptional fee refund where appropriate.

A marketplace fee will not be retained where doing so would violate a mandatory refund entitlement.

9. Reporting a Problem

Customers should report suspected problems as soon as reasonably possible.

Reports may be submitted through Returns & Disputes or another authorized Shop&Drop support channel.

Relevant information may include:

  • The Shop&Drop order or booking reference.
  • The product or service listing reference.
  • The agreed item description or service scope.
  • The courier tracking or collection reference.
  • The date and nature of the complaint.
  • Relevant photographs or supporting evidence.

Evidence should be submitted using an authorized private channel rather than publicly displaying sensitive information.

Users must not disclose security PINs or another person's confidential details.

A failure to report immediately does not extinguish statutory rights that remain available.

10. Dispute Review and Resolution

Shop&Drop is designed to automate ordinary transaction processing and qualifying refunds where supported by an approved payment provider.

Two authorized administrators handle genuine exceptions, complaints, suspected fraud and disputed evidence.

Product condition or description disputes ordinarily involve the independent buyer and seller.

Courier loss and transportation claims ordinarily involve the responsible carrier under its applicable agreement.

Shop&Drop remains responsible for its own applicable intermediary obligations.

Where lawful and necessary, administrators may facilitate direct communication or disclose limited contact information under the Privacy Policy.

Such disclosure is not automatic and must be proportionate to the legitimate purpose.

A complaint does not automatically establish that either party is correct. The relevant evidence and applicable rights must be considered fairly.

11. Seller and Provider Payout Protection

Physical goods: The intended payout milestone is independently verified physical collection by the authorized courier, following confirmed payment.

The planned verification process may use a seller-held collection PIN, courier scanning, the correct shipment reference and an authenticated courier collection confirmation.

The buyer's later delivery PIN is separate and is not intended to be the normal condition for releasing the product seller's proceeds.

A seller's unsupported claim that the courier collected a parcel is insufficient.

Other Services: Payout eligibility follows verified completion of the agreed service, supported by appropriate authenticated completion evidence or confirmation PIN.

Dispatch and equipment: Payout eligibility follows verified destination handover, transport completion or the end of the agreed paid equipment working period, as applicable.

Eligible payouts should be processed automatically without an arbitrary routine 24–48-hour waiting period.

However, confirmed payment, fraud safeguards, qualifying disputes, mandatory financial holds and provider requirements may affect release.

Shop&Drop must prevent duplicate payouts and unauthorized releases.

These revised payout milestones remain prelaunch and inactive until successfully integrated, approved and tested.

12. Refund Processing

Where a refund is legally or contractually due, the responsible party and authorized payment provider must handle the applicable refund process.

Shop&Drop intends to automate routine qualifying refund instructions where supported by the provider.

Exceptional cases may require investigation and administrator review.

A seller or provider having already received funds may affect the recovery process, but does not automatically end a lawful claim.

Refund timing depends on the payment method, banking arrangements and payment-provider procedures.

Shop&Drop will not guarantee a refund settlement time or recovery outcome that has not been confirmed.

Customers will receive relevant refund status notifications through supported channels.

Live refund processing remains subject to payment-provider activation and testing.

13. Fraud and Misuse

Fraudulent advertisements, false courier-collection claims, fabricated scans, misleading completion photographs, misuse of six-digit PINs and fraudulent disputes are prohibited.

Shop&Drop may retain relevant evidence, apply lawful transaction safeguards, investigate exceptional cases or refer unlawful activity to appropriate authorities.

Legitimate customer complaints and genuine seller or provider payment claims must not be dismissed merely because investigation is necessary.

14. Consumer Rights

Applicable South African consumer protection laws may include the Consumer Protection Act and the Electronic Communications and Transactions Act.

The application of particular rights depends on the parties, the circumstances and any statutory exceptions.

Shop&Drop's intermediary role does not automatically remove its own statutory obligations.

Mandatory rights cannot be excluded merely because an item is second-hand, the seller has already received payment or the buyer did not read a policy.

Nothing in this policy excludes a remedy or legal protection that cannot lawfully be waived.

15. Policy Updates

This policy describes Shop&Drop's intended prelaunch intermediary and payment arrangements.

The approved payment-provider terms, courier arrangements, actual backend behavior, effective date and contact details must be aligned before public transactions are activated.

Material changes will be published and communicated where legally required.
